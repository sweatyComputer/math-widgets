The ball is a perfect mirror under one softbox and a grey studio. Its colour at each point comes from the measured data at that viewing angle.

How to use these. In a reflectivity + edge-tint node, type in Reflectivity and Edge tint (ACEScg) and you're done. A node that takes n and k wants the fitted n,k row: those reproduce the full-spectrum colour at every angle. The measured n,k row is the raw data at three single wavelengths and renders copper and brass noticeably too red.

How the colour changes with angle and wavelength

Reflection vs. viewing angle

Looking straight at a metal (0°) shows its own colour. Tilt it towards edge-on (90°) and every metal goes white. Edge tint controls how it gets there.

RedGreenBlue
Per angle θ: Fresnel reflectance of the complex index N = n + ik, averaged over both polarisations, computed for each wavelength, then turned into ACEScg red, green and blue.

Reflection vs. wavelength (straight on)

How much of each colour of light bounces back when you look straight at it. Flat means grey (steel); a curve rising towards red means warm (copper, gold).

F0(λ) = ((n−1)² + k²) / ((n+1)² + k²) at every wavelength λ from 380 to 780 nm.

Can you build an alloy from its ingredients?

Pick a real alloy with measured data. Each row fakes it from the pure metals a different way, and scores how different the fake would look next to the real thing. Under 2 is a near-perfect match; over 5 you'd see it side by side; over 10 it's a different colour. Change the percentages to test other recipes.

What each method does

Main metal only
Ignore the minor ingredients and use the biggest one.
Average n and k
Blend the n and k numbers by percentage. What most alloy guides do.
Average permittivity
Blend a related quantity (ε, how the metal's electrons respond to light) instead of n and k.
Grain mixture
Treat the alloy as tiny grains of each metal packed together (the Bruggeman model).
Average reflectivity
Blend the final colours directly.
Under the hood. Average n,k: N = Σ fᵢ Nᵢ. Permittivity: ε = Σ fᵢ Nᵢ², N = √ε. Bruggeman: solve Σ fᵢ (εᵢ − ε)/(εᵢ + 2ε) = 0. Reflectivity: F0 = Σ fᵢ F0ᵢ. Score: CIEDE2000 colour difference between the faked and measured straight-on colour under daylight.

Why brass fails every method: alloying changes how copper's electrons absorb light, so brass ends up yellower than both copper and zinc. No blend of two ingredients can land outside them.

Edge tint generator

For a shader where artists paint reflectivity: the edge tint is worked out from their value with one fixed number per channel, t, the share of the gap to white the edge closes. Pick which metal t comes from, then drag the reflectivity.

The node graph. edge = r × (1 − t) + t, which is the same as mix(r, 1, t). Then reflectivity r and edge go into the reflectivity + edge-tint node, which rebuilds n and k (Gulbrandsen 2014): n_min = (1−r)/(1+r), n_max = (1+√r)/(1−√r), n = g·n_min + (1−g)·n_max, k = √((r(n+1)² − (n−1)²)/(1−r)).

Plain-English glossary

Reflectivity, F0
How much light a surface bounces back when you look at it straight on, per colour. 1 is a perfect mirror. Steel is about 0.6, silver about 0.97.
Fresnel effect
Surfaces reflect more as you look at them closer to edge-on. Metals already reflect a lot, so the change is subtle, but they all go white at the very edge.
n and k
The two numbers physicists use to describe how a material bends and absorbs light. n is the bending, k the absorbing. Metals have big k. Each changes with the colour of light, which is what gives metals their colour.
Edge tint, g
An artist-friendly stand-in for n. Together with reflectivity it fully defines n and k. It mostly shapes the colour near grazing angles.
t
How far the edge tint sits between reflectivity and white. 0 means the same as reflectivity, 1 means pure white. Iron is about 0.1, silver about 1.
Wavelength, nm
The colour of a single ray of light, in nanometres. Violet is about 400, green 530, red 650. Our eyes see roughly 380 to 780.
Spectrum
A value for every wavelength. Real reflection is a spectrum; a renderer squashes it into three numbers (red, green, blue).
D65
The standard recipe for average daylight that colour scientists use. All colours here assume the metal is lit by it.
CIE 1931 observer
A standard table of how sensitive an average human eye is to each wavelength. It turns a spectrum into a colour the way a person would see it.
XYZ
The raw colour response of that standard eye. Every RGB space is defined from it.
ACEScg
The wide-gamut RGB working space most film pipelines render in. Values here include the white-point adjustment from daylight to ACES white (Bradford), so a perfect white is (1, 1, 1).
Linear sRGB
The standard screen colour space, before the gamma curve. Used here only to show colours on screen.
ΔE, CIEDE2000
A score for how different two colours look to a person. About 1 is barely visible, 2–3 a close match, 5 clearly different, 10+ a different colour.
F82 tint
Another edge-colour control used by OpenPBR and Adobe's model, based on reflection at 82°. Included for those shaders.

Data sources and caveats

All n,k data comes from the refractiveindex.info database (Polyanskiy 2024). Each metal's source is shown under the picker.

  • Thin films read darker. Johnson & Christy data is from thin evaporated films. Chromium film is about 15% darker than polished chrome. Where a Rakić version exists, prefer it.
  • Zinc from Querry is excluded. It gives n ≈ 13 in the visible, which is physically implausible. Werner's zinc is used instead.
  • Werner datasets are partly computed (density functional theory), not purely measured.
  • Mixing compositions are typical, not exact: stainless austenitic as Fe72 Cr18 Ni10, ferritic as Fe83 Cr17, permalloy as Ni80 Fe20. Edit them in the test.
  • Surface finish usually matters more than any of this. Roughness, polish and grime easily outweigh a ΔE of 3.

Colour maths: CIE 1931 2° observer and D65 at 5 nm steps, XYZ → ACEScg via Bradford adaptation, CIEDE2000 per Sharma et al. 2005. Fitted n,k: reflectivity from the full-spectrum ACEScg colour at 0°, edge tint fitted per channel to the full-spectrum colour at every angle (Gulbrandsen 2014 remap).
